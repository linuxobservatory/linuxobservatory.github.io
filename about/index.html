ABOUT / THE PROJECT

An active observatory.
Not a passive archive.

Linux Observatory is an independent, ongoing technical audiovisual project that examines real-world Linux gaming behavior through hands-on experimentation and instrumented gameplay.

THE IDEA

Testing is the product.

The work begins before the video exists: selecting a workload, configuring a Linux graphics stack, enabling appropriate instruments and running the game.

The recording preserves that observation so others can see its context. Publication is not merely storage; it gives a test a stable public record and allows other people to inspect its behavior.

The project is independently operated by Ediz Iniziel in Copenhagen, Denmark. It uses an unsponsored, unscripted recording style and does not present every gameplay video as a controlled benchmark.

The guiding rhythm is Observe. Instrument. Record. Inspect.

PRINCIPLES

What this project does—and what it doesn't claim.

01

Visible measurement

Show instrument readouts alongside the game when instrumentation is available and appropriate.

02

Conditions matter

A result belongs to the specific game, hardware, driver, runtime, settings and resolution—not every computer.

03

Limits are part of the record

A crash, failure or 6 FPS run can be interesting without being declared a universal verdict.

04

No false causality

An observed hitch is real evidence of a hitch; its exact cause needs further work.

05

No borrowed authority

No invented awards, collaborators, industry validation or generalized benchmark claims.

06

Public primary source

The footage remains available on the official YouTube channel for direct inspection.

This site intentionally avoids a fabricated founding date or claims about audience size. A history page can be expanded as its timeline is documented.

THE FOOTAGE IS THE EVIDENCE.

See it for yourself.

A single run documents what happened under specific conditions. Watch the recording. Inspect the on-screen metrics. Ask better questions.

THE EDGE OF THE POSSIBLE / RESOLUTION STRESS

Why test a game
at 16K or 32K?

Not because it is a sensible way to play. Raising internal rendering resolution dramatically increases the amount of pixel work and may expose driver, memory, allocation and application constraints.

PUT THE SCALE IN PERSPECTIVE

The number of pixels is the story.

Resolution target pixel counts and ratios relative to 4K
Example targetDimensionsPixels per frameRelative to 4K
4K (UHD)3,840 × 2,1608.3 million1×
8K7,680 × 4,32033.2 million4×
16K15,360 × 8,640132.7 million16×
Experimental 32K target32,768 × 18,432604.0 million72.8×

Pixel counts are exact products of the stated dimensions, displayed rounded. “32K+” here refers specifically to the project-reported 32,768 × 18,432 target—not one universal 32K standard.

WHY IT MATTERS

Memory pressure

One uncompressed 32-bit color image at 32,768 × 18,432 takes about 2.25 GiB before depth buffers, extra render targets, textures, copies, alignment and other overheads.

WHAT IT DOES NOT MEAN

Not automatically a 32K display

A virtual render size can exceed the physical monitor resolution. Gamescope and other mechanisms may scale the final output down to the actual display.

A failed 16K attempt is still an observation—not a diagnosis.

If a run terminates, the recording can document the failure. Establishing whether VRAM exhaustion, an API limit, a driver bug or another factor caused it requires additional evidence.
